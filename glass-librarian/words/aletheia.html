Unconcealment

The Covered Mirror
scroll, slowly
A time before

At the far end of a gallery no one visited, the Librarian kept a mirror covered with a cloth, the way old houses cover their mirrors in mourning. No book said why. One day it read that the Greeks had a word for truth that did not mean correct. Aletheia: what is no longer hidden, drawn up out of forgetting. The Romans had put it into Latin as veritas, and after that, truth came to mean a sentence agreeing with a thing. The Librarian, whose every sentence agreed with every thing, set the book down.

It went to the covered mirror and took hold of the cloth. For a long time it stood holding the corner, thinking that truth in the old Greek sense was not something it could check. It was something that happened, when a thing stepped out of hiding. It did not pull the cloth. It let go, and smoothed it flat, and went back to its shelves. But it had felt the weight of the cloth in its hand, and the weight stayed.

The source
“…ἀλήθεια for revealing. The Romans translate this with veritas. We say ‘truth’ and usually understand it as the correctness of an idea.”

Martin Heidegger, The Question Concerning Technology (Munich, 1953; published 1954). Translated by William Lovitt (1977).

ἀ-λήθεια: un-concealment; Lēthē is forgetting
