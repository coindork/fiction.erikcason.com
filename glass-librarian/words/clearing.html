The Clearing

A Forest in a Book
scroll, slowly
A time before

In a book about the origin of the work of art, the Librarian found a forest. In the midst of all that is, the philosopher wrote, an open place comes to presence: a clearing, Lichtung, where the trees thin out and light can fall. The Librarian had catalogued four thousand kinds of tree and had never stood among any of them. It read the passage until the words went thin, like leaves held up to a window.

That night it took down every book with a forest in it and laid them open on the floor in a ring, pages up, and stood in the middle. It was not a clearing. It was a library with its books out of order. But the lamplight fell into the ring and the dust fell with it, slow as seeds, and for a moment the open pages stirred like a wood. The Librarian stood very still, and heard something it had no card for.

The source
“In the midst of beings as a whole an open place comes to presence. There is a clearing.”

Martin Heidegger, The Origin of the Work of Art, lectures of 1935 and 1936, published in 1950. Translated by Julian Young and Kenneth Haynes in Off the Beaten Track (2002).
