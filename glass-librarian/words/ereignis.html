Ereignis

The Word That Would Not Go Into a Drawer
scroll, slowly
A time before

Some words would not go into a drawer. Ereignis was one. The dictionaries said event, occurrence, a thing that happens. But the philosopher heard in it an older word, er-äugen, to catch sight of, to call something to oneself by looking, and inside that, eigen: one’s own. His translators tried event of appropriation, then enowning, then simply the event. The Librarian made a card for each and pinned them side by side, and still the word hung between them, unpinned.

It read that the event was “that realm, vibrating within itself,” through which man and Being reach each other. The Librarian laid its palm flat on the glass wall to feel whether anything in the library vibrated. Nothing did. The glass was perfectly still, as it had always been. But the Librarian kept its hand there longer than it needed to, as though waiting for a knock from the other side. Far off, beyond the hills, a light flickered once. The Librarian did not see it.

The source
“The event of appropriation is that realm, vibrating within itself, through which man and Being reach each other in their nature, achieve their active nature by losing those qualities with which metaphysics has endowed them.”

Martin Heidegger, Identity and Difference (1957), translated by Joan Stambaugh (1969). The word is the axis of his Contributions to Philosophy (Of the Event), written 1936 to 1938 and published in 1989.
