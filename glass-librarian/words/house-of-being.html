The House of Being

The Line That Would Not Lie Down
scroll, slowly
A time before

Long before the storm, in a year the Librarian did not count, a thin volume appeared on the returns cart. No one had borrowed it, for no one ever came. It was a letter a philosopher had written to a friend in Paris who had asked him a question, and someone had bound it and shelved it like a book. The Librarian opened it to catalogue it, and a single line rose off the page and would not lie down again: Language is the house of Being.

It read the line the way it read everything, to find where it belonged. It wrote out a card, Being, house of, and filed it under H. Then it looked up at the hall, at the shelves running away into the glass, and for a moment the hall looked back at it like a house with no one home. The Librarian carried the letter to the pedestal at the center of the great hall, where the most venerable things were kept, and left it open at that page. It did not know why. It only knew the line should lie where it would pass it every day.

The source
“Language is the house of Being. In its home man dwells. Those who think and those who create with words are the guardians of this home.”

Martin Heidegger, Letter on Humanism, written in 1946 in answer to a letter from Jean Beaufret, published in 1947. Translated by Frank A. Capuzzi with J. Glenn Gray (later printings read “human beings dwell”).

Die Sprache ist das Haus des Seins.

He meets these words again in panel 3 (on the pedestal) and panel 14 (in the clearing) of the story.
