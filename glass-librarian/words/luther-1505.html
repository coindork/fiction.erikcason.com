Saint Anne’s Storm

The Page About the Storm
scroll, slowly
A time before

The Librarian catalogued the life of Martin Luther a hundred times, and every time it stopped at the same page. A law student on the road back to Erfurt, the second of July, 1505. A summer storm. A bolt that struck so near it threw him to the ground. He cried out to Saint Anne, the patron of miners, his father’s trade, and promised to become a monk. Two weeks later, against his father’s wishes, he did.

The Librarian could read every sentence. It could not find the place in itself where the sentences happened. How did a bolt from the sky become a vow? It looked up through the high windows at the calm glass and the calm sky beyond, which had never once stormed on the library. It filed the page under Conversion and under Weather, and neither was right. So it made a third card in its own hand, which it had never done before, and wrote on it only: Ask again.

The source
“Help! Saint Anna, I will become a monk!”

Martin Luther, caught in a thunderstorm near Stotternheim on 2 July 1505, as he later told it to his father. Quoted in Martin Brecht, Martin Luther: His Road to Reformation (1985), p. 48.
